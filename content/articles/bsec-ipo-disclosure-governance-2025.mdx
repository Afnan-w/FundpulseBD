The Bangladesh Securities and Exchange Commission has strengthened disclosure and governance requirements covering initial public offerings (IPOs), listed companies, and mutual funds.

For mutual funds, the 2025 regulations require trustees to hold legal ownership of fund assets while unit holders remain beneficial owners. Custody of assets and money is placed more firmly under custodians, separating asset management from direct control over cash and securities. Asset managers make investment decisions, while custodians execute payments for securities purchases and receive proceeds from sales. Management fees claimed by asset managers are subject to custodian verification before payment.

Funds must also submit detailed quarterly reports on portfolio holdings, disclose income, expenses, risks, and valuations regularly, and may not conceal price-sensitive information.

For IPOs and listed companies, BSEC continues to emphasize timely publication of financial reports and compliance with disclosure norms. Companies and issuers should refer to the latest BSEC circulars and the Bangladesh Capital Market rules for current procedures and penalties for delayed or incomplete disclosures.

These measures aim to protect investors, improve market transparency, and reduce the risk of fund diversion or mismanagement.
