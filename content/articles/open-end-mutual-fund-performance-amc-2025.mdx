Open-end mutual funds are not listed on the stock exchange. Instead, investors buy or sell units directly from the asset management company (AMC) at the fund's prevailing net asset value (NAV). NAV is calculated by dividing the total value of the fund's assets minus liabilities by the number of units outstanding.

Performance of an open-end fund depends on the underlying portfolio—typically a mix of listed equities, government securities, and other permitted instruments—and on fees charged by the AMC. Under BSEC's 2025 rules, fund managers must publish daily NAV and disclose income, expenses, risks, and portfolio valuations regularly, improving transparency for unit holders.

Bangladesh's asset management industry includes several AMCs managing both open-end and closed-end funds. Industry data indicates open-end funds collectively make up a growing share of total mutual fund assets, though closed-end funds still represent a significant portion of listed fund market capitalization.

Before investing, readers should review the fund's trust deed, prospectus, fee structure, historical NAV trends, and dividend policy. Past performance does not guarantee future results, and mutual fund investments carry market risk.
