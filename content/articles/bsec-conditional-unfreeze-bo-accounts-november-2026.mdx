The Bangladesh Securities and Exchange Commission (BSEC) has conditionally lifted the freeze order on 93 beneficiary owner (BO) accounts held by seven individuals, including stock market investor Abul Khayer Hiru and members of his family. The order, signed by BSEC Chairman Masud Khan, was issued on 13 November 2026 following a commission meeting.

The accounts had been frozen on 21 August 2024, after the change of government, as part of investigations into alleged market misconduct. According to the schedule attached to the order, Hiru's 32 accounts make up the largest share, followed by Kazi Sadia Hasan with 23, Sayadur Rahman with 11, and Konika Afroze with 10, with the remainder spread across three other individuals.

Conditions attached

The unfreezing takes effect only if the accounts are not subject to separate freeze or restriction orders from courts, the Anti-Corruption Commission, the National Board of Revenue, the Bangladesh Financial Intelligence Unit, or any other authority. Central Depository Bangladesh Limited (CDBL) will verify account status before executing the order.

For eligible accounts, the conditions are strict:

- Only regular secondary-market trading is permitted. Share transfers, transmissions, and block-market transactions are prohibited.
- Account holders may adjust margin financing under the Margin Rules 2025.
- Withdrawal or transfer of funds from the accounts remains barred.
- Trading does not apply to shares under pledge, lien, or lock-in.

The DSE, CSE, and CDBL must ensure compliance and submit monthly reports to the commission by the 10th of each following month.

Wider context

This order follows a 3 November decision in which the regulator eased restrictions on selected accounts among the roughly 617 BO accounts frozen over the past two years, allowing buying, selling, and fresh deposits while keeping cash withdrawals blocked. Market commentary has credited the gradual reopening of these accounts — reportedly more than 100 so far — with helping improve sentiment on the Dhaka Stock Exchange.

The conditions are designed to let frozen investors resume normal market participation without letting capital leave the market while investigations continue. Further unfreezing decisions are expected case by case.
