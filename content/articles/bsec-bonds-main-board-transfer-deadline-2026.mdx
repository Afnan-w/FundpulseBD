The Bangladesh Securities and Exchange Commission (BSEC) has directed that bonds move from the Alternative Trading Board (ATB) to the main boards of the country's stock exchanges. The decision was announced on 10 November 2026 through a press release from the regulator.

Who is affected

- Issuers of bonds whose consent letters specified ATB listing as a condition but which have not yet listed there must apply for main-board listing by 30 November 2026.
- Stock exchanges must complete the transfer of bonds already listed on the ATB to their main boards by the same deadline.

Applications will be considered, and transfers completed, in line with existing listing regulations and applicable listing requirements.

Why this matters

The ATB was introduced as a lighter-touch listing venue for bonds and other instruments. In practice, activity there has remained thin, and several market participants — including the president of the DSE Brokers Association, speaking to BSS in early November — argued that moving roughly Tk 35 billion worth of bonds onto the main board would lift overall market turnover and give retail investors a visible, actively traded fixed-income segment.

A main-board listing also places bonds alongside equities in the ordinary trading screens, making it easier for savers to see quoted prices and trade volumes. Bond trading carries interest rate and credit risks that differ from shares, and prices fall when market interest rates rise. Readers should check the terms of each bond, including coupon, maturity, and the issuer's financial condition, before buying.

The broader context

This move sits alongside other BSEC decisions taken in the same commission meeting — a single-window IPO review system and an extension of the negative equity adjustment deadline for intermediaries to 2030 — as part of a wider set of market-structure reforms announced in November 2026.
