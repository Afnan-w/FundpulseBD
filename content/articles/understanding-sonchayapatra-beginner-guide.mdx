Sonchayapatra (savings certificates) are government-backed savings instruments offered through the National Savings Directorate (NSD). They are designed to encourage safe savings among individuals in Bangladesh.

Common types include Sanchaypatra, Pensioner Sanchaypatra, and Paribar Sanchaypatra, each with different eligibility rules, tenure, and interest rates. Interest rates are set by the government and are periodically revised; new purchases generally earn the rate in effect at the time of purchase.

To open an account, a saver typically needs to visit a designated bank branch or post office with required documents, complete an application form, and nominate a beneficiary. Encashment follows NSD rules and timelines, which vary by scheme.

Existing certificate holders continue to earn their originally contracted rates even if new rates are announced later. Savers should confirm current rates, documents required, and encashment procedures at an NSD office or authorized bank before investing.
