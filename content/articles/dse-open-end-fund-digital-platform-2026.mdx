The Dhaka Stock Exchange (DSE) is preparing to launch a dedicated digital platform that will let investors buy, sell, and redeem units of open-ended mutual funds online, with a go-live target around mid-November 2026.

The problem it solves

Today, investing in an open-end fund in Bangladesh is largely an offline affair: investors must visit the office of the fund manager (asset management company) to purchase or redeem units. That friction is one reason retail participation in mutual funds remains low despite more than 100 open-end funds operating in the market. The proposed platform moves the order-management process online: investors will be able to view each fund's performance and place orders through their existing stockbrokers, with the brokers facilitating purchase and redemption through the system.

DSE Managing Director Nuzhat Anwar described the platform as a step towards making mutual fund investment easier, more convenient, transparent, and technology-driven.

Market context

- There are about 106 open-end mutual funds, with assets under management of roughly Tk 58 billion as of August 2026.
- Around 34 closed-end funds are listed on the DSE, with AUM of about Tk 43 billion.
- BSEC amended the mutual fund regulations in May 2026 to lay out how closed-end funds can convert into open-end funds — with at least 75% of unitholders' approval — which has been reshaping the fund landscape.

What this could change

If the platform works as intended, buying a fund unit becomes as simple as placing a trade through a broker — no branch visits, with published NAV and performance data visible before ordering. For savers earning shrinking fixed-deposit returns, easier access to professionally managed funds — including fixed-income funds that have posted returns in the 9–10% range — could widen their options.

Caveats worth knowing

- Mutual fund units are not bank deposits. Open-end funds can be equity-heavy or fixed-income, and NAV can fall.
- Fund performance varies widely between managers; past returns do not guarantee future results.
- Fees (management and other charges) reduce returns, and dividend taxation for open-end funds differs from that on listed shares — an inequality commentators have criticised.
- The platform's rollout details — which funds participate, settlement timelines, and fees per transaction — will matter as much as the launch itself.
