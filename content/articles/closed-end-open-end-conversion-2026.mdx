Under the Mutual Fund Regulations, 2025, existing closed-end mutual funds cannot extend their tenure. Funds that fail to maintain their market value in line with the rules may be required to convert into open-end funds or face liquidation.

Currently, 37 closed-end mutual funds are listed on the Dhaka Stock Exchange, with terms expiring by 2032. Many of these funds have traded at discounts to their net asset value for extended periods, limiting investors' ability to exit at fair value.

Open-end funds differ in that investors can buy or sell units directly from the fund manager at any time based on the fund's NAV, rather than trading on the stock exchange. BSEC has said it will facilitate the conversion of closed-end funds into open-end structures so investors have a better opportunity to realize the value of their holdings.

The regulator has also taken action against poorly performing asset managers, including changes to management appointments, to improve the quality of fund management. These measures are part of a broader effort to strengthen governance and custody of fund assets.

Investors holding units in closed-end funds should monitor official communications from their fund manager and trustee, and review the terms of any conversion or liquidation proposal carefully.
