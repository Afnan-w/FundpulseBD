Bangladesh's mutual fund industry remains small relative to the size of the economy. Total assets under management (AUM) across the sector stand at approximately Tk 10,186 crore, with closed-end funds holding about Tk 4,810 crore and open-end funds about Tk 5,376 crore. The industry's AUM-to-GDP ratio is estimated at around 0.17%, the lowest in the region.

The Dhaka Stock Exchange lists 37 closed-end mutual funds, many of which have traded at substantial discounts to net asset value (NAV). Reports indicate that between 2024 and 2025, nearly every closed-end fund lost between 30% and 150% of its value, and by late 2025 the market capitalization of closed-end funds had fallen to roughly Tk 2,312 crore. Of the listed closed-end funds, the majority trade below NAV and a significant number trade below face value.

BSEC has identified mutual fund reform as a priority. Under the Mutual Fund Regulations, 2025, the regulator is phasing out new closed-end approvals, strengthening trustee and custodian oversight, requiring daily NAV publication, and facilitating the conversion of struggling closed-end funds into open-end structures. The stated goal is to rebuild investor confidence, protect unit holders, and improve transparency in portfolio holdings and fees.
