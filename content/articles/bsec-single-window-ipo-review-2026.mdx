The Bangladesh Securities and Exchange Commission (BSEC) has decided to introduce a single-window review system for initial public offering (IPO) applications. The decision was taken at a commission meeting on 10 November 2026, chaired by BSEC Chairman Masud Khan.

Under the new arrangement, stock exchanges will take the lead in reviewing IPO applications. They will incorporate the commission's observations alongside their own findings, then submit consolidated recommendations to BSEC for final approval. This brings the scrutiny done by the exchanges and by the commission into one coordinated process.

According to the regulator, the system is expected to reduce procedural complexity for companies that want to list, remove duplication in the review chain, and shorten the time from application to approval.

Why it matters

Bangladesh's primary market has seen an IPO drought of nearly three years. The regulator's move comes as six companies have recently submitted IPO applications seeking to raise a combined Tk 6.12 billion. A faster, clearer review path could encourage more issuers to come to market, which would widen the list of investable companies for savers who want exposure beyond fixed deposits and savings certificates.

What prospective issuers and investors should know

For companies, the practical change is that applications will be processed through the relevant stock exchange rather than moving separately between the exchange and the regulator. For investors, faster approvals do not change the risk of any individual IPO — the quality of each issuer still varies, and listing rules and disclosure requirements continue to apply.
