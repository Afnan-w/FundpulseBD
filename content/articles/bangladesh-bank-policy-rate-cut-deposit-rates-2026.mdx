Bangladesh Bank entered a new phase of monetary policy in 2026. After holding its policy rate at 10% since October 2024 to fight inflation, the central bank cut the rate to 9.50% on 30 July 2026 (effective 2 August), its first reduction in nearly two years. It then kept the rate unchanged at its September review, citing domestic and global shocks including volatile energy prices and a domestic fuel price hike.

The policy backdrop

Bangladesh Bank has also moved to a quarterly Monetary Policy Statement — the October–December 2026 issue was its first. In it, the central bank noted that inflation had eased somewhat after the long restrictive stance but remained above the medium-term target, while economic growth had slowed under weak domestic and external demand. The bank's forecasts put headline inflation around 8.9% by December 2026, moderating to about 8.6% by June 2027.

Why savers should care

Deposit rates follow the policy rate down, with a lag. Bangladesh Bank's published data show the weighted average interest rate on deposits around 9.26% as of early November 2026, but averages hide a wide spread between banks. Reporting in October 2026 noted that one-year fixed deposit rates at several leading banks had fallen to around 7.5% — below the September inflation reading of about 8.3%, meaning a negative real return for savers at those banks.

Practical points for fixed-deposit holders

- The rate printed on your FDR certificate is not necessarily the rate you keep. Effective yield can be reduced by how interest is compounded and paid, and by source tax deducted on interest.
- Banks are repricing deposits at different speeds. Comparing rates across several banks before renewal is worthwhile, as is checking whether a "special" scheme rate applies.
- Government-backed savings certificates (Sanchayapatra) continued to pay 11.77%–11.98% at maturity through December 2026, after the government kept rates unchanged — though purchase limits, source tax, and eligibility rules apply.
- Lower policy rates, if they lead inflation to fall faster than deposit rates, could restore positive real returns over time — but that is a forecast, not a certainty.

What to watch

The next quarterly monetary policy statement, monthly inflation prints, and the government's six-monthly savings rate notifications will signal where returns on savings head into 2027.
